Wye Valley Honey & Ginger Sparkling Mead Review

Wye Valley Honey & Ginger Sparkling Mead combines floral honey with warming ginger in a light, highly carbonated Welsh session mead. Made by Hive Mind Mead & Brew Co., formerly Wye Valley Meadery, it offers the sweet-and-spicy appeal of a ginger drink without the weight of a rich, wine-strength mead.

Our verdict: bright honey, brisk bubbles and a clean ginger glow make this refreshing rather than heavy. Its spice is warming and persistent rather than brutally hot, while a dry finish keeps the opening sweetness in check. It is an approachable sparkling metheglin for cider, ginger beer and Prosecco drinkers.

At a glance

  • Producer: Hive Mind Mead & Brew Co., formerly Wye Valley Meadery
  • Origin: Welsh Borders, Wales
  • Style: Ginger metheglin and sparkling session mead
  • Strength: Currently listed at 3.4% ABV; some earlier stock may show 4%
  • Pack size: Currently sold in 330ml cans; Master of Malt lists a 33cl format under the Wye Valley name
  • Sweetness: Honeyed initially, becoming drier through ginger and carbonation
  • Serve: Well chilled, ideally around 8–12°C

Wye Valley Meadery was founded in 2018 by brothers Matt and Kit Newell before becoming Hive Mind Mead & Brew Co. The sparkling range modernises mead through a lower strength, small cans and lively carbonation, while fermented honey remains central to the drink.

Hive Mind currently lists Honey & Ginger at 3.4% ABV in 330ml cans. Older examples and retailer listings may use Wye Valley Meadery branding, different packaging or a 4% label, so the container in front of you is the best guide to a particular production run.

Appearance

In the glass it is brilliant and clear, ranging from very pale straw to light gold. The colour is delicate enough to suggest a dry sparkling drink rather than a dark, syrupy honey wine.

Carbonation is lively. Tight streams of bubbles rise through the glass and can briefly build a crisp white head before it disappears. The persistent bead gives the mead a sparkling-wine appearance and helps prepare the palate for a much lighter texture than a traditional still mead.

Nose

Honey arrives first, with flowers and bright wildflower-nectar character rather than caramel or heavy cooked sweetness. Ginger follows immediately: fresh, aromatic and warm, with the sharp lift associated with ginger oils.

There can also be an impression of lime zest or citrus pith around the spice. This is best understood as a tasting note rather than a declared separate ingredient. The overall aroma is clean and invigorating, without the dense alcoholic scent that can accompany stronger meads.

Taste

The opening is crisp and zesty. Carbonation prickles across the tongue, lifting the honey and preventing its sweetness from settling into a sticky texture. Floral honey provides the rounded middle of the sip before ginger begins to tighten and warm the palate.

The balance is more important than sheer heat. Ginger supplies a distinct kick, but it is cushioned by smooth honey rather than becoming aggressive. The spice feels fresh and gently fiery, making this closer to a grown-up sparkling ginger drink than a chilli-hot mead.

Its light-to-medium body suits the current 3.4% strength. The texture is sparkling and wine-like, yet the 330ml can and sessionable alcohol level make it equally natural as an alternative to cider. It remains recognisably mead because the floral sweetness sits beneath the fizz and spice throughout.

Finish

The finish is cleaner and drier than the first honeyed impression suggests. Sweetness recedes quickly, leaving fresh ginger warmth and a pleasant fiery tingle across the back of the palate.

Carbonation helps the finish stay brisk rather than syrupy. A faint citrus-pith impression may remain alongside the ginger, but the lasting feature is spice: warming enough for cold weather, while still refreshing when served chilled in summer.

Is Honey & Ginger a metheglin?

A metheglin is a mead flavoured with herbs or spices, so ginger places this drink comfortably within the broad metheglin family. Its low alcohol, carbonation and can format are modern, but the underlying combination of fermented honey and spice has deep roots in mead making.

That does not make it a ginger beer. Ginger beer may be alcoholic or alcohol-free and is not defined by fermented honey. Here, honey is the mead base rather than simply a sweetener added to a conventional soft drink.

What is it made from?

Hive Mind describes the drink as mead infused with root ginger and says its sparkling meads are brewed on honey rather than white wine. The current product page declares water, honey, natural flavouring and yeast.

The distinction between description and ingredient declaration matters. Although the producer refers to root ginger, the published ingredient list does not separately name it and should not be rewritten as only honey, water, yeast and ginger. Nor does an absence of grain from the published list replace formal allergen or gluten-free certification. Anyone avoiding a particular ingredient should check the current can or contact Hive Mind about the latest formulation.

How to serve Honey & Ginger Sparkling Mead

Serve well chilled, around 8–12°C, in a flute, stemmed wine glass or small beer glass. A flute preserves the bead, while a wider glass releases more honey and ginger aroma. Pour carefully because the first burst of foam can be lively.

Do not serve it so cold that the floral aroma disappears. The lower end of the range emphasises crispness; allowing it to warm slightly brings out more honey and ginger. A 330ml can works naturally as one long serving.

It drinks well on its own as an aperitif or as a change from cider and Prosecco. A wedge or twist of lime can reinforce the perceived citrus edge, but taste it unadorned first so that the honey-and-ginger balance remains clear.

Food pairings

Ginger warmth, honey sweetness and cleansing bubbles make this particularly useful with aromatic or spicy food:

  • Thai and Vietnamese dishes: lemongrass, herbs and moderate chilli echo its fresh, zesty character.
  • Sushi or lightly spiced seafood: ginger is a natural partner, while carbonation refreshes the palate.
  • Chinese roast pork or duck: honey complements caramelised meat and ginger cuts through richness.
  • Grilled chicken: especially with lime, coriander or a light soy glaze.
  • Summer salads: try crisp leaves, cucumber, citrus and fresh herbs rather than a heavy dressing.
  • Mild ginger cake or poached pear: choose a restrained dessert so it does not overwhelm the mead’s dry finish.

Avoid pairing it with extreme chilli heat if you want to notice the subtler floral honey. Moderately spiced dishes produce a more balanced contrast.

How it compares with other Wye Valley meads

Wye Valley Meadery Traditional Mead is richer, stronger and designed for slower sipping. Honey & Ginger is lighter and more refreshing, using carbonation and spice to balance sweetness rather than alcoholic weight.

Wye Valley Honey & Rhubarb Sparkling Mead offers a sharper fruit-led sweet-and-sour contrast. Wye Valley Honey & Elderflower Sparkling Mead is softer and more floral, while Wye Valley Honey & Hops Sparkling Mead finishes with citrus bitterness. Choose Ginger when you want the range’s warmest, most obviously spiced expression.
