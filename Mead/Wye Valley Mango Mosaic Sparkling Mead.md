Title: Wye Valley Mango Mosaic Sparkling Mead Review
Summary: Wye Valley Mango Mosaic Sparkling Mead review with tropical tasting notes, Mosaic hops, 4% ABV, serving advice and UK availability.
Tags: [[Welsh Meads]], [[Sparkling]]

Wye Valley Mango Mosaic Sparkling Mead was a tropical, hop-influenced session mead from the producer now known as Hive Mind Mead & Brew Co. Pairing mango with Mosaic hops and a honey-fermented base, it drew inspiration from juicy modern IPAs without becoming a conventional malt beer.

**Our verdict:** an exuberant combination of mango, pineapple-like fruit, floral honey and crisp hop bitterness. Vanilla and brown-sugar-like impressions add softness through the middle, but carbonation and Mosaic hops keep the finish zesty. Its tropical character makes it one of the most beer-adjacent and distinctive releases from the former Wye Valley range.

## At a glance

- **Producer:** Hive Mind Mead & Brew Co., formerly [[Wye Valley Meadery]]
- **Origin:** Welsh Borders, Wales
- **Style:** Mango and Mosaic-hopped [[Sparkling|sparkling session mead]]
- **Strength:** 4% ABV
- **Pack size:** 330ml/33cl bottles or cans, depending on the release
- **Sweetness:** Fruity and honeyed, balanced by carbonation and hop bitterness
- **Current status:** A previous flavour, absent from Hive Mind's current online range when checked
- **Serve:** Well chilled

Brothers Matt and Kit Newell founded Wye Valley Meadery in 2018 before combining the meadery and brewing sides of the business under the Hive Mind name. Mango Mosaic belonged to the producer's early wave of modern sparkling meads, designed to be enjoyed in the same kind of occasion as craft beer or cider.

A later account of a visit to the meadery described Mango Mosaic as one of its previous flavours, with only a few bottles remaining at that time. Hive Mind does not currently list it in its sparkling-mead collection, so it should be treated as a discontinued or dormant release rather than part of the dependable core range. Master of Malt still carries a product listing under the Wye Valley Meadery name, but stock can change.

## Appearance

Mango Mosaic pours a light golden-straw colour, brighter and paler than its tropical flavour might suggest. It looks like a modern sparkling mead rather than a thick mango drink or dark honey wine.

Steady carbonation brings fine bubbles and a light head that settles quickly. The sparkle keeps the presentation lively and reinforces the session-strength character of the drink.

## Nose

Ripe mango leads the aroma, juicy and tropical rather than jammy. Pineapple-like notes sit alongside it, giving the nose the broad, highly aromatic fruit character associated with modern hop-led drinks.

Mosaic hops contribute citrus, flowers and additional tropical lift. Wildflower honey remains in the background, with impressions of vanilla and light brown sugar providing a rounder counterpoint to the bright fruit. Vanilla, brown sugar and pineapple are tasting notes here, not a verified list of added ingredients.

## Taste

The opening is a burst of zesty mango: the "juice bomb" idea used in descriptions of the release is immediately understandable. Tropical fruit arrives before the honey, with pineapple-like brightness widening the flavour beyond a single mango note.

The middle of the sip becomes smoother. Floral honey supplies body and sweetness, while creamy vanilla and brown-sugar-like impressions make the fruit feel riper and rounder. Those darker notes never turn it into a heavy dessert mead because the body remains light and the carbonation continues to lift the palate.

Mosaic hops provide the balancing turn. Their citrusy, floral bitterness cuts across the fruit and honey, preventing the drink from becoming cloying. The bitterness is recognisable to craft-beer drinkers but does not require malt to perform its role; the fermented honey remains the drink's base.

At 4% ABV, Mango Mosaic was made as a long, session-style drink rather than a wine-strength mead for small measures. It bridges tropical fruit mead and juicy IPA flavour territory while retaining a softer honeyed centre.

## Finish

The finish is zesty and refreshing, with tropical fruit receding into gentle hop bitterness. Mango and pineapple-like notes persist, followed by a lighter echo of floral honey.

A little vanilla-like creaminess and brown-sugar warmth may remain after the bubbles fade. These are flavour comparisons rather than evidence that vanilla, pineapple or brown sugar was added to the recipe.

## Is Mango Mosaic a beer, braggot or hopped mead?

Its inspiration came from New England-style IPA, and Mosaic is a hop variety widely associated with citrus and tropical-fruit aromas. That makes the sensory comparison with hazy modern beer useful, but inspiration does not make Mango Mosaic an IPA.

A braggot normally combines honey with malted grain or beer. No reliable ingredient declaration located for this release establishes a malt component, so the safer classification is **mango and Mosaic-hopped sparkling mead**. Hops alone do not turn mead into beer or braggot.

## What was it made from?

Available product descriptions establish a honey-fermented sparkling-mead base with mango and Mosaic hops. Hive Mind says its meads use British honey, including honey from its own Wye Valley bees and trusted British beekeepers, rather than white wine sweetened to resemble mead.

However, a complete batch-specific ingredient declaration for Mango Mosaic was not available from the current producer site. It would therefore be inaccurate to present mango, pineapple, vanilla and brown sugar as a verified ingredients list: several of those are retailer tasting notes. Anyone buying remaining stock should use the bottle or can label as the controlling source for ingredients and allergens.

## How to serve Mango Mosaic Sparkling Mead

Serve well chilled in a stemmed beer glass, wine glass or small tulip. A glass releases the hop and mango aroma more effectively than drinking directly from the container, while leaving enough room for the initial foam to settle.

Avoid serving it near-frozen, which would suppress the honey and tropical aroma. It should be cold enough for the carbonation and bitterness to feel crisp but warm enough for mango and Mosaic to remain expressive.

The 330ml format suits a single long serving. There is little need for ice, as dilution would weaken the hop-and-fruit balance that gives the mead its identity.

## Food pairings

Tropical fruit, honey and hops make this a natural partner for food with spice or caramelisation:

- **Aromatic curry:** mango sweetness softens moderate chilli while hop bitterness refreshes the palate.
- **Thai food:** lime, herbs, coconut and gentle heat complement its tropical profile.
- **Jerk chicken:** char, spice and fruit connect with the mead's sweet-bitter balance.
- **Grilled pork:** especially with a mango, pineapple or chilli glaze.
- **Fish tacos:** citrus, coriander and crisp slaw suit the hops and carbonation.
- **Salty cheese:** young Cheddar or goat's cheese offers contrast without overwhelming the fruit.

Avoid very sweet mango desserts, which can flatten the drink's fruit and make its hop bitterness seem harder.

## How it compares with other Wye Valley meads

[[Wye Valley Pure Honey Sparkling Mead]] is simpler and more directly floral, providing the clearest view of the honey base. Mango Mosaic adds tropical fruit and a more obvious craft-beer influence.

[[Wye Valley Honey & Hops Sparkling Mead]] is the closest relative, but its Citra-led lemon and lime character is leaner than Mango Mosaic's broad tropical profile. [[Wye Valley Honey & Rhubarb Sparkling Mead]] balances honey through tart fruit rather than hops, while [[Wye Valley Honey & Ginger Sparkling Mead]] uses warming spice.

Choose Mango Mosaic when tropical fruit and modern hop aroma matter most. Choose Pure Honey when you want to understand the unflavoured sparkling-mead foundation.
