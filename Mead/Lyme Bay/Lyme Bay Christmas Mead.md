Lyme Bay Christmas Mead is a sweet, gently spiced honey wine designed for colder evenings and festive food. It is richer and more seasonal than the winery’s standard [traditional mead](/lyme-bay-traditional-mead/), without becoming a spirit-strength mead liqueur.

## Quick verdict

**Best for:** people who enjoy sweet dessert wines, warming spice and honey-led drinks.

**Style:** still Christmas mead / spiced honey wine.

**Strength and size:** the official listing showed **10% ABV in a 75cl bottle** when checked on 11 October 2026.

**Overall:** an accessible Christmas bottle with enough spice to feel festive and enough honey character to remain recognisably mead. Drink it in small pours, serve it with cheese or pudding, or warm it very gently.

## Taste

Lyme Bay describes the bottle as a rich honey wine blended with Christmas spices. Expect honeyed sweetness first, supported by warming baking-spice character rather than the sharp bitterness of a heavily botanical drink.

Serving temperature changes the balance. Chilling restrains sweetness; a cool room-temperature pour brings out more aroma; gentle warming emphasises the comforting spiced character.

This is not the bottle for someone seeking a bone-dry table wine. For a first-time buyer who likes port, dessert wine, mulled wine or sweet liqueurs, it is an easier recommendation.

## How to serve it

Try one of three approaches:

1. **Cool room temperature:** pour a small measure into a wine glass and let it open for a few minutes.
2. **Lightly chilled:** useful when the sweetness feels too rich.
3. **Gently warmed:** heat slowly in a pan until pleasantly hot, without simmering or boiling.

Do not boil mead: heat drives off volatile aromas and alcohol. There is no need to add sugar. Taste before adding orange peel, cinnamon or cloves because the wine is already spiced.

## Food pairings

Lyme Bay Christmas Mead suits:

- mature Cheddar or blue cheese;
- mince pies and fruit cake;
- Christmas pudding;
- baked apple or pear desserts;
- roast ham and other sweet-savoury dishes;
- toasted nuts and dried fruit.

A small serving works best with rich food.

## How it compares

Unlike Friary’s currently verified Christmas liqueur—which is made with mead, French brandy and spices—Lyme Bay’s product is sold as Christmas Mead/honey wine at a wine-like 10% ABV. Read labels carefully because “Christmas mead”, “mead liqueur” and “honey liqueur” are not interchangeable categories.
